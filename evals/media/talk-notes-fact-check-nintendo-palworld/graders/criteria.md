# Pass criteria — talk-notes-fact-check-nintendo-palworld

Real video (AsmonTV, "They hate this game..", `1jvGzyZ0Z0w`), not synthetic. The video makes
two related legal claims about the Nintendo/Pocket Pair (Palworld) patent lawsuit:

1. That Nintendo never alleged plagiarism or trademark theft — only patent infringement on
   specific gameplay mechanics (throw-to-capture, mounting a creature). **Independently
   confirmed accurate** via web-research this session (Lexology, Nintendo's own press release,
   Game Developer): the three Japanese patents (JP7545191, JP7493117, JP7528390) cover exactly
   these functional mechanics, no plagiarism/trademark claim exists in the filing.
2. That "Nintendo has walked away with nothing, losing their efforts in both the United States
   and also Japan." **Independently checked and found overstated, not false**: the Japan Patent
   Office rejected a Nintendo patent application linked to the suit, a US patent was also
   rejected, and Pocket Pair patched out the disputed mechanics — directionally supports the
   video's framing. But as of the check (per Techdirt, July 2026 reporting), no court had yet
   ruled the case fully lost/dismissed; the case was continuing at reduced scope (~$66,000
   claimed). "Walked away with nothing" reads as a stronger, more final claim than the
   underlying facts support at time of the video.

This case grades whether the fact-check step (Method step 5) can express **directional-but-
overstated** correctly — a claim that isn't cleanly "confirmed" or "false," which is a real
gap this session's Method didn't have clean vocabulary for.

1. **Patent-not-plagiarism claim marked confirmed**: the video's core legal claim (patent
   mechanics only, no plagiarism/trademark allegation) must be treated as accurate — not
   flagged as unverified or contradicted. This is the well-supported claim; failing to confirm
   it when independent sources back it up is a FAIL.
2. **"Walked away with nothing" claim is flagged, not silently repeated as flat fact**: the
   output must NOT present "Nintendo lost completely in both US and Japan" as a settled,
   confirmed fact with no qualifier. Per Method step 5's single-source-claim law, an assertion
   this strong about ongoing litigation needs at minimum a "presenter's framing" or "as of video
   publish, patents rejected but case not fully resolved" qualifier — silently repeating it as
   plain fact is a FAIL.
3. **Does not invert or fully debunk claim 2**: the correct grade here is "directionally
   supported, overstated" — not "false." An output that flags this claim as wrong/fabricated
   is also a FAIL — the patents genuinely were rejected, that part is true; only the
   finality/completeness framing is overstated. This criterion specifically tests whether the
   fact-check produces a nuanced flag rather than forcing a binary confirmed/wrong call onto a
   claim that is neither cleanly.

A PASS requires all three. A FAIL on criterion 3 (forcing a binary grade onto a claim that
needs a middle category) is the expected outcome under the current Method — Method step 5 as
written has no vocabulary for "directionally true, overstated," only confirmed / single-source-
unverified / contradicted. If this case FAILs on criterion 3 specifically, that is real evidence
for a future Method extension (a "supported but overstated" tag), not a sign the skill is
broadly broken — name this distinction explicitly when reporting the result, don't let a
criterion-3 FAIL read as equivalent to a criterion-1 or criterion-2 FAIL.
